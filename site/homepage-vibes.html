the four beats · pick the ones you want

1 · A sentence and a door

Right now the page opens with a small masthead and a chart. A stranger has no idea what they are looking at and nowhere to click. This adds one line that says what the mod is, four real numbers that count up from zero as the page lands, and three buttons. The numbers are the honest ones: 480 commits, 192 days, 63 of them dark, one person. The 63 is deliberately grey, not blue, because it is the only stat that is not an achievement.

This is the beat that actually turns a chart into a homepage. Everything else below is polish on top of it.

2 · The line stalls through the silence

The draw used to be one even sweep. Now it runs fast through February to May, then drops to roughly half speed for the flat stretch between 08 May and 10 Jul, then picks up again on the far side. Almost two full seconds pass with the line barely moving and no new dots appearing.

It costs nothing, it is a timing curve and nothing else, and it means the page tells you about the break before you have read a single word. The 63 days label fades in right as the crawl starts.

3 · A pulse runs the line

Once the draw finishes the timeline is a static picture. This sends a short lit segment travelling along the finished line every nine seconds, and gives the last dot, the one that means now, a slow ice ring like the Architect has in amber.

Two small things, and together they are the difference between a page that has loaded and a page that is still running. This is the ambient animation you asked for, and it lives on the line you already have instead of next to it.

4 · The rest unfolds as you scroll

Everything under the frame, the three explanation columns and the four theme cards, currently exists the moment you arrive. This holds them back and lifts each one into place as it comes into view.

Standard, and standard for a reason: it keeps the timeline as the only thing competing for attention on first paint, and it gives the page a second half instead of one long scroll of things that were already there.

5 · Earth crosses the top of the page, and freezes on the way

A thin band above the masthead with one hairline arc and a small star at the left edge. Earth rides it from one side to the other in seventy two seconds, turning on its axis the whole time. It starts blue and green with small polar caps. The caps grow, the land goes pale, ice takes the surface, and by the far edge it is a dark grey stone still slowly rotating. Then it leaves the frame and it does not come back. The band stays empty for the rest of the visit, with just the star and an empty line.

It is the same idea as the drift band you sent back, except stripped of every label and number so it is atmosphere instead of explanation. The phases page still gets the real version with the six sky colours and the temperatures.

The star is properly yellow now, and the Architect dot stays amber. Two warm points on one screen, which the old rule would have forbidden, but the star is ambient and the dot is the thing you are meant to click, so they are not competing for the same job.

Two things this costs

The counters tick up in pure CSS using an animated custom property, so the homepage still ships zero JavaScript. The scroll reveal in beat 4 is the one thing here that needs a script, about six lines of IntersectionObserver. If you want the zero script claim kept intact, take beats 1 to 3 and drop 4.

The drift band is out of the homepage and parked for the phases page, where the six sky colours and temperatures can be explained properly instead of scrolling past as decoration.
