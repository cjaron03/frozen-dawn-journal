Two things are worth noticing before you read any further. The first is that the warm area has a hard edge. There is no falloff curve. The second is that in phase five, taking the roof off a heater is worse than dropping a whole tier.

Four heaters. The difference between them is two numbers, and one block sitting next to them doubles both.

A heater is not a machine that warms the air around it. It is a radius and a constant. If the block you are standing on is inside the radius, you get the whole constant. If it is not, you get nothing at all. That decision is one line, and it never changed after the day I wrote it.

The Thermal Capacitor is the single best block in the survival tree and it does not look like it. It doubles the radius and multiplies the heat by one and a half. A Thermal heater with a capacitor reaches further than a Diamond heater without one, which is deliberate: the upgrade path is meant to have a shortcut in it for players who find the capacitor first.

The whole decision, TemperatureManager.javaJava
// one tier, unrolled. the other three are the same shape. if (state.is(ModBlocks.THERMAL_HEATER.get()) && state.getValue(ThermalHeaterBlock.LIT)) { int baseRadius = hasCapacitor ? 14 : 7; if (phase >= 5 && !sheltered) baseRadius = Math.max(2, (int) (baseRadius * 0.6f)); baseRadius = Math.max(2, baseRadius - radiusPenalty); int maxDistSq = baseRadius * baseRadius; float heat = hasCapacitor ? 52.5f : 35.0f; return distSq <= maxDistSq ? Math.max(0.0f, heat - heatPenalty) : 0.0f; }

I have gone back and forth on the hard edge more than once. A smooth falloff is more realistic and it is also completely unreadable in play: you would never know whether moving two blocks helped. The binary version teaches itself. Players work out the exact radius within an hour of getting a heater and then build their base around it, which is the behaviour I wanted.

Shelter in this mod is not a mood. It is a loop that looks up.

I wanted a player to be able to work out, without a wiki, whether the thing they just built counts. So the check is the dumbest thing that could possibly work: stand where you are, look straight up four blocks, and if any of them is solid, you are sheltered. Five degrees. That is the whole function.

getShelterModifier, TemperatureManager.javaJava
public static float getShelterModifier(Level level, BlockPos pos) { for (int dy = 1; dy <= 4; dy++) { BlockPos above = pos.above(dy); BlockState aboveState = level.getBlockState(above); if (aboveState.isSolidRender(level, above) || aboveState.is(ModBlocks.INSULATED_GLASS.get())) { return 5.0f; } } return 0.0f; }

Insulated glass is the exception written directly into that condition, and it is the reason the block exists. Glass is not solid, so ordinary glass fails the check and a greenhouse is just a cold box with a view. Insulated glass is the one transparent block that counts as a roof, which lets you build somewhere you can actually see out of without giving up the five degrees.

Four blocks up, not one. You should be able to put a lamp in your ceiling without losing your shelter.
A roof, and then walls

Five degrees is the cheap version. The expensive version is enclosure, and it is what most of the later systems actually test for. A position is enclosed when it has a roof within four above it and a solid block in all four cardinal directions within ten. Insulated glass counts for the walls too.

Ten blocks is generous on purpose. It means a reasonable room passes and an open pavilion does not, without forcing anyone to build a cupboard.

The phase five rule

Everything above is background for one line that changed how the mod plays. From phase five onward, a heater with nothing over it keeps only sixty percent of its radius. Not its heat, its radius, which means the area falls to about a third.

The tuning note I left myself, TemperatureManager.javaJava
// In phase 5+, exposed heaters (no roof) have 60% radius (distSq * 0.36). // This ensures Diamond exposed (r~8.4) > Base enclosed (r=7).

That second line is the whole balance argument. The penalty had to be harsh enough that roofing your heater is never optional, and gentle enough that a player who has spent the diamonds is still ahead of a player who has not. Sixty percent is the number where the best exposed heater just barely beats the worst sheltered one. Anything lower and the tech tree stopped mattering in the endgame.

The result in play is that phase five is when everybody's base changes shape. Open-air heater arrays that worked for thirty days stop working in a single night, and you find out because the readout drops, not because anything told you.

In phase six there is no air outside. The game has to decide, constantly, whether the room you are in counts as inside.

This is the check I rewrote the most times, because it is the only one where being wrong kills someone who did nothing wrong. It is a flood fill. It starts on the block you are standing in, walks outward through every air block it can reach in all six directions, and asks one question of each: can this block see the sky? If any of them can, the room is not sealed and you are breathing vacuum.

hasBreathableAir, TemperatureManager.javaJava
public static boolean hasBreathableAir(Level level, BlockPos pos) { if (level.dimension() != Level.OVERWORLD) return false; if (hasOxygenSupport(level, pos)) return true; return isInsideSealedRoom(level, pos); } public static boolean hasOxygenSupport(Level level, BlockPos pos) { return BlastPitWarmZoneRegistry.isInsideWarmZone(level, pos) || isInsideGeothermalO2Range(level, pos); }

Note the order. Oxygen support is checked first and it short circuits, because a blast pit warm zone or a geothermal core is supposed to make the flood fill irrelevant. Those structures produce air on their own terms, and running a twelve thousand block search to confirm something the registry already knows would be a waste of a tick.

The four ways it says no
SKYAny visited block can see the sky. This is the honest failure, and it is the one players cause themselves.
BOUNDSThe fill wandered more than 48 blocks horizontally or 24 vertically from where you are standing. A tunnel network is not a room.
BUDGET12,000 blocks visited and still going. At that size it is cheaper to call it open than to keep looking.
UNLOADEDThe fill reached a chunk that is not loaded. Unknown counts as open, because guessing the other way suffocates people.

Every one of those is a refusal to guess in your favour, and that is intentional. The cost of a false positive is a player who sealed their base, walked in, took the helmet off, and died. The cost of a false negative is a player who has to wear a helmet in a room that probably would have been fine. Only one of those is recoverable.

Unknown counts as open. If the algorithm cannot prove you are safe, it says you are not.

The bounds do a second job that is easy to miss. Without them, a player who digs a long mineshaft has a single connected air volume the size of a chunk, and every check anywhere in it costs the full budget. Capping the search at 48 blocks turns an unbounded question into a local one, and the fill collections get reused between calls rather than reallocated, because this runs often enough that the garbage would show up.

Frostmites do not attack you. They attach to the thing keeping you alive and then they wait.

Every survival system above is a wall between you and the cold. A wall is only interesting if something is pushing on it. Frostmites are that push, and the design brief I gave myself was that they should never deal damage directly. They should make your own equipment stop working and let the world finish the job.

How they find you

A frostmite targets within fourteen blocks, but it will cross eighteen for a lit heater. Heaters are bait, and they outrank you. Six can latch onto a player at once and ten onto a heater, and once attached they stick for a hundred and forty ticks on a player or two hundred on a heater before they will even consider letting go.

TARGET_RANGE 14How far it will look for anything at all.
HEATER_BAIT_RANGE 18How far it will come for a lit heater specifically. Your base is louder than you are.
MAX_HEATER_LATCH 10Ten at once, which is exactly enough to hit every cap simultaneously.
IS_FREEZINGFreezing damage is rejected outright. You cannot kill a frostmite with the cold.

That last one closed an exploit I liked losing. Frost Ward Torches and freezing effects were the obvious answer to a swarm, and they trivialised it, so the entity now refuses freezing damage entirely and clears its latch when it takes any other hit. The intended answer became the MiteAway burner, a block that costs fuel and space and does nothing else, which is the trade I wanted: defending your heat should cost you some of it.

What I would do differently

The heater tiers should have been three, not four. Iron and Gold do the same job at different prices and neither of them changes how you play, which makes the middle of the ladder a wait rather than a decision. If I were starting again the jump would be Thermal, then Gold, then Diamond, and the capacitor would stay exactly where it is.

The seal check I would leave alone. It is the slowest thing in the mod per call and I have tried three times to make it cleverer, and every version that was faster was also wrong in some room shape I had not thought of. Being conservative and occasionally rude about a room is the correct answer.
