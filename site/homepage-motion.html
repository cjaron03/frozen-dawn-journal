A · Ambient, behind the header

A single hairline arc above the masthead. Earth crosses it once every 54 seconds, shrinking and cooling as it goes, and the only warm pixel on the page is the Sun at the far left. No labels, no explanation. Most people never consciously notice it, and the ones who watch for ten seconds see the whole premise of the mod. Cheapest option, and it leaves the timeline completely untouched as the centrepiece.

B · The Drift band, under the timeline

A second band below the commit line, drawn with the same grammar: one hairline that draws itself while Earth rides it out of orbit. The six stops are the six real phases, and the band tints to the actual sky colour of each one as Earth reaches it, so the strip goes from warm amber to pure black over 26 seconds. It is the only place on the site where the fiction gets to speak in its own voice, and the Sun is its single amber object. My recommendation.

C · Scrub it yourself

Same band, except you drag Earth along the path and the readout and the sky colour follow your finger. It turns the phase system into something you operate instead of something you watch. The cost is honest: this is the first JavaScript on the homepage, which breaks the zero-script promise the homepage currently keeps. Worth it only if you want the drift to be a toy rather than a mood.

Project Lighthouse, at the foot of the page

The seven ORSA relay satellites, with their real call signs and their real causes of failure straight out of the RS-04 black box. Three still pulse. A signal line crosses the row every eleven seconds and lands on Mars, which is the only other amber object on the site. It closes the homepage on the same note the mod ends on: someone is still listening, and they are very far away. My recommendation, and it doubles as a footer.

Frost creeping in from both corners

Frost creep on the frame

Ice grows inward from the corners of the timeline frame and a faint diagonal hatch fades up behind everything, breathing over about nine seconds. No new content and nothing to read, so it never competes with the timeline. It is atmosphere rather than information, which makes it the safest option and also the least memorable one.

Snow settling into a drift along the base

The snow starts to settle

The snow already falling on the homepage stops passing through the floor and begins to pile against the bottom edge of the frame, rising a few pixels the longer you stay. It rewards sitting still, which is a strange thing to ask of a homepage, and the payoff is small unless someone lingers. Reuses the twenty six snow divs that already exist, so it costs almost nothing.

What in here is real

The six colours are the literal phase targets in SkyRenderer.java, converted from float to hex: warm amber, pale desaturated, cold blue, blue purple, near black purple, pure black.

The six temperatures and phase names are from the Projected Earth Timeline entry in the guide book, written by the ORSA Science Division.

22 June is the date Heliostatic Correction Initiative 4 activated, on the summer solstice, the longest day. The board approved it 7 votes to 2 with an internal destabilisation risk of 94 percent and a published figure of 15 percent.

The seven relays and their causes of failure are from the RS-04 black box log. Three are still in passive listening mode waiting for a deep band transponder.
